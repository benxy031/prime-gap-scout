CROSS-MACHINE VERIFICATION OF THE PHASE0 GPU OUTPUT
==================================================
Same Scout 1.3.5 source, same window, two different cards and two different architectures.

  window    [1e24, 1e24 + 1e14)
  threshold gap >= 1134  (merit 20.5 at 1e24: minimum_gap(10^24) = 1134)
  reference RTX 5070, sm_120, Windows, CUDA driver 13.050
            scout_report_start_1000000000000000000000000.txt, 432 gaps, 127.0 s
  ours      RTX 3070, sm86, Linux, CUDA 12.4
            pgn68-worker-sm86-k32 --engine walk --start 1e24 --length 1e14
            --device 0 --gap-min 1134 --emit-min-gap 1134
            432 gaps, 215.598 s

RESULT

  gaps reported by the 5070        432
  gaps reported by the 3070        432
  identical (gap, lower) pairs     432
  missing from ours                  0
  extra in ours                      0
  max |merit difference|       0.000000

Every lower prime and every gap length agrees exactly. The endpoints of both sides are proven
with the same in-worker gate (Euler composite interior, EXACT_ENDPOINTS_EULER_COMPOSITE), so this
is not two implementations agreeing on a screen - it is the same proof obligation satisfied
independently on two architectures.

WHAT IT ESTABLISHES

1. The phase0 GPU output is reproducible across architecture. sm86 and sm_120 produce the same
   answer over 1e14 positions at 2^80, with zero divergence.
2. Speed for that window: 127.0 s on a 5070, 215.6 s on a 3070, ratio 1.70 - consistent with the
   cards' difference in shader throughput, and consistent with the width sweep measured earlier
   (0.463 T/s here at 80 bits).
3. For scale, the same window on a CPU sieve of the Atlas class runs at ~0.3 G positions/s, i.e.
   roughly 3.9 days against 2-4 minutes. That factor, not the code, is the argument.

HONEST SCOPE

This verifies the sieve, the walk and the gap enumeration. It does not exercise Atlas's base-prime
streaming, its PGS2 output, or its research tooling, and it says nothing about licensing.
